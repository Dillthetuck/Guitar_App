# First topic: beginner teaching plus open practice

November 6, 2026. Lead-owned planning draft. Existing decisions: low-E/A sample; open available topics; optional tests; beginner teaching paired with endless practice. Exact teaching package, layout and build acceptance below are recommendations awaiting review. This is not product implementation authorization.

## A small, complete sample

Propose two short teaching samples: E/F/G on low E and A/B/C on A. Then mixed find/name practice. This replaces the earlier tentative four-lesson packaging for the first interaction review; it does not redefine a full natural-notes course as six taught positions. Remaining natural notes and higher frets can be added after the interaction is accepted. Three-to-five minutes is a target to measure, not a promised duration or requirement to pad a lesson.

## Screen flow

| Screen | Main content and actions |
|---|---|
| Topic entry | Fretboard notes. Learn the basics / Practice now. Quick check is optional and visually secondary. No signup or test before the first activity. |
| Teaching | One explanation or worked example at a time, then a guided action. Back/exit always available. |
| Question | One task; recognizable six-string neck; active-string emphasis; naming buttons or direct fret input; optional help. |
| Result | Immediate correct/incorrect feedback; clear explanation after a mistake; explicit Continue where feedback needs reading. No lives cost for an error in this proposed sample. |
| Session recap | What was practiced; independent-first versus assisted responses; Practice these / Next sample / Leave. No mastery declaration. |
| Return | Suggested short review or next teaching sample, alongside direct topic/practice access. |

An experienced player's route is Topic → Practice now → first question. Start with a visible preset, such as Low E/A, first three frets, sample notes; settings are optional. No long questionnaire. In a future expanded app, opening another available topic follows the same rule.

## Teaching sample one

Objective: locate/name open E, F at fret 1 and G at fret 3 on the thickest string.

1. Optional orientation: identify thickest string, open position and numbered frets. Explain that the picture is a map and how high/thin versus low/thick strings are arranged. No assumed tab literacy.
2. Explain that note letters run A through G and repeat. A letter alone is not always one fret away; some intermediate pitches have sharp/flat names. Do not teach the false rule that every next letter is one fret higher.
3. Worked example: open E → F at fret 1. Say that E–F are neighbors, one half step apart; a half step is one fret.
4. Guided find F; show help when requested, classify demonstrations/reveals honestly.
5. Worked example: F → F♯ → G at frets 1/2/3. Briefly explain ♯ as a half step higher. F♯ is introduced as a stepping stone, not silently included as an assessed target.
6. Vary finding and naming E/F/G without target labels. Interleave at least one other item before retesting a revealed answer; do not equate changed order with delayed recall.
7. Recap the two relationships and offer scoped practice. The user can leave at any point.

## Teaching sample two

Objective: locate/name open A, B at fret 2 and C at fret 3 on the next thickest string.

1. Optional low-E recap or direct start; no required completion of sample one. Offer an inline explanation of unfamiliar fret/half-step terms.
2. Show A → A♯ → B: two frets. Introduce A♯ as an intermediate pitch, not an assessed target by default.
3. Show B → C: one fret. Contrast with A–B. This reinforces the E–F/B–C exception without an entire chromatic-alphabet lecture.
4. Guide, then mix finding/naming A/B/C. Introduce cross-string practice explicitly before mixing with E/F/G.
5. Offer practice on this sample, both samples, or another available scope. Default reinforcement includes only material the player chose to practice/was taught, never silently broadening scope.

First sample uses natural-note target names only. In finding tasks, restrict accepted positions to the indicated string and stated range. In mixed practice make the string constraint explicit; multiple equivalent pitches must not become an accidental wrong-answer trap. Notes outside the declared question range are not active answer targets. The full neck is context, not an invitation to tap any valid octave.

Intermediate frets inside the declared range remain tappable distractors: low-E fret 2 and A fret 1 trigger useful correction, but are not requested naming targets. Store question format, string/fret, first answer and all help/correction exposure. Unhinted recall of a previously demonstrated position is immediate retrieval, not unseen-content transfer.

## Practice and help

Finding example: “Find G on string 6.” Naming example: highlight string 5/fret 2; “Name this note.” Early naming choices use the relevant three taught letters, with positions/order varied. A larger mixed answer set is introduced explicitly.

All six strings remain visible. Independent questions use neutral string numbers and visual thick/thin cues; note labels are removed when they would disclose the requested answer. In particular, an open E naming question cannot claim uncued retrieval while displaying E beside that string. Guided mode may show those labels, but records that support.

Wrong-answer correction names the tapped pitch and shows how to reach the target. If a sharp is unfamiliar, provide its plain-language meaning. This assistance makes subsequent correction assisted, even if the help button was never used. A fresh question can later provide independent-first evidence; it does not erase the earlier mistake.

Requested help: relationship cue → worked path → direct reveal. Each stage records actual information shown. No stage number automatically qualifies as independent. Offer a brief return to teaching and preserve the chosen practice scope. Never require watching the whole lesson to resume practice.

Endless practice continues until the user leaves. A proposed daily session is 8 questions, emphasizing encountered items with mistakes/help, mixing naming and finding and avoiding immediate duplicate prompts where alternatives exist. Provide a recap at 8 and Continue practicing. This count and selection are provisional, not a proven retention schedule. No background notifications or mandatory new-topic work are included.
