## Design objective

Make answering on the fretboard feel immediate, readable and playful on iPhone 11. Use a small teaching sample to exercise instruction, correct/incorrect feedback, help, naming, recap and return. Do not produce many lessons before reviewing these states.

Preserve confirmed preferences: original guitar-pick character, short wording, visual path, quick interactive puzzles and real guitar audio. Keep all six strings visible as orientation context, consistent with Dylan's prior prototype request; only the relevant string(s) accept answers. Low-E/A teaching scope does not imply a two-string guitar graphic. Assess whether a closer fret view is needed rather than retaining twelve cramped columns automatically.

## Representative first lesson: E to F to G

Proposed objective: locate and name E, F and G on low E within frets 0–3, using the no-gap E–F relationship. This is deliberately narrower than all natural notes on one string.

| State | Example wording/action | Evidence captured |
|---|---|---|
| Orientation | Highlight low E. “This is the thickest string. Open means no fret pressed.” Tap its open note. | Guided activity only |
| Explain | “Each fret moves one half step. E → F is one fret.” Briefly animate open E to fret 1. | Exposure, not recall |
| Guided find | “Find F on low E.” Open E stays labeled; show a count-one cue if help requested. | First response and help exposure |
| Extend | “F → G takes two frets: F → F♯ → G.” Show positions 1, 2, 3 briefly, then remove answer labels. | Exposure |
| Find | “Find G on low E.” | Independent first response versus correction/help |
| Name | Highlight low E fret 1. “Name this note.” Choices E / F / G, order varied. | Different task format; still immediate practice |
| Mixed practice | Ask open E and fret 3 in changed order, without initial answer labels. | Immediate independent practice, not delayed mastery |
| Recap | “E–F: one fret. F–G: two.” Separate completed work from unhinted first answers. | Honest summary |

Wrong-answer example: tapping fret 2 for G gives “That’s F♯. G is one fret higher.” This reveals useful answer information and must mark the corrected attempt as assisted. Next use a fresh item before claiming an independent response. A direct answer reveal remains available without punishment.

Independent fixture: low E open E2, fret 1 F2, fret 2 F♯2/G♭2, fret 3 G2. Proposed audio needs actual pitch/octave identifiers. The open position must be selectable for activities that ask for it, unlike the recovered prototype.

The proposed next A-string sample uses A2/B2/C3 at frets 0/2/3. The lesson count and eventual natural-note coverage need packaging after timing checks; do not force all notes into an overloaded four-lesson plan.

## Interaction decisions to resolve with review specimens

- Compare a closer fret window with a whole-neck overview against the existing twelve-column board. Preserve string orientation and clearly show the visible fret range. Do not choose on appearance alone: verify tap accuracy on the phone.
- Recommend one tap submits an answer, instant result feedback, then an explicit Continue after instructional feedback. Compare automatic advance for already-familiar review items; never remove feedback before it can be read or announced.
- Show one primary instruction. Put optional explanation/help behind a clear action. Avoid a long chromatic alphabet on every question.
- Preserve visible focus and announce results without using color alone. Respect reduced motion and mute. Character reactions encourage effort without falsely announcing mastery.
- Keep initial visual exploration bounded: two directions using the same lesson/state content, then Dylan chooses what to refine. Current cream/purple/pick styling is reference material, not final approval.
