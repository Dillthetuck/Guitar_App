# Product vision

## Dylan's current intent
“Duolingo for guitarists”: real, bite-sized, applicable guitar theory presented as a playful daily habit. Serve newer guitarists and players who want theory but never find time for it during practice. Learning should work away from a guitar and eventually transfer to playing one.

The desired reasons to choose it are ease of use, motivation, effective learning, visible progress, and strong marketing. These are aspirations to validate, not established advantages. Earlier research identified direct competitors; neither market demand nor profitability is proven.

## Experience
- Lessons target 3–5 minutes and build into larger units.
- A visual learning road and familiar, playful navigation organize progress.
- Available topics can be opened without mandatory prerequisite completion or test-out. A recommended path and optional placement/checks support navigation without gating access.
- Each overarching exercise area should pair direct practice with an optional beginner lesson series/walkthrough. Someone who does not know note names should be able to learn through guidance, then reinforce through endless practice. Exact topic/release coverage and pricing remain open.
- The fretboard is the main interactive surface: find or name notes, locate chord shapes, construct triads, identify intervals; ear training is a later candidate.
- Endless practice reuses lesson activities and should target meaningful review.
- Short explanations, fast transitions, responsive feedback, and readable touch targets address Dylan's earlier complaints: heavy wording, too many clicks, clunky puzzles, and insufficient playfulness.
- Confirmed November 6: portrait navigation with landscape exercises; rotating feels natural to Dylan.
- Confirmed visual direction: supplied pale-yellow/yellow/orange/red-orange palette, blue textured minimalist line-art references, and typography/lines with that handmade character. Harder exercise levels move toward red-orange. Exact typeface, graphics and final screens await review; see VISUAL-DIRECTION.md.
- Characters are guitar picks or guitar-themed characters, with personas inspired by genre and vibe; no animal mascots.
- Dylan wants the familiar feel of Duolingo's path and playful presentation. Earlier he explicitly wanted an original visual identity rather than a complete copy. Develop original characters, artwork, wording, and styling, and have Dylan resolve the desired degree of resemblance.
- Guitar sounds should come from real guitar recordings. Dylan may record samples later. Sample scope, string/fret timbre, octaves, file naming, loudness, looping, loading, latency, rights, and mute behavior need planning. Pitch-class labels alone cannot identify an audio sample's octave.
- Streaks are a central preference because Dylan values repeat use for learning and potential revenue. Streak maintenance alone does not establish retention, learning, or business value.
- Premium is undecided; Duolingo-like benefits are a reference, not a purchased implementation or confirmed pricing model.
- Earlier guitar legends/culture ideas can support theory, but are secondary to the current theory-first direction.

## Success hypotheses to test
1. A target player can begin and finish a lesson without explanation.
2. Lesson timing lands near the intended 3–5 minute range in observed use.
3. The player can answer fresh questions without the original hints.
4. Some learning transfers to a real guitar and persists after a delay.
5. Players voluntarily return; streak incentives support rather than displace useful learning.
6. Revenue can eventually exceed acquisition and operating costs.

Define measures and continuation criteria with Dylan before testing. Do not invent results, testimonials, target conversion rates, or mastery claims.
